Unit 01 · HT1

Ghost Boys

Seven weeks with Jewell Parker Rhodes' Ghost Boys (2018): Jerome, twelve and Black, is shot by a white police officer in Chicago while holding a toy gun, and as a ghost he can be seen by only one living person — Sarah, the officer's daughter. We read the whole novel in class, learn the words for prejudice and injustice, follow Jerome and Sarah towards empathy and allyship, meet the ghost of Emmett Till, and ask what it means to bear witness.

01

What you will learn

Three aims, climbing in difficulty. Every lesson in this unit works towards one of them.

UNDERSTAND

Explain the key terms — prejudice, injustice, inequality, stereotype, discrimination — and retell Jerome's story from the shooting to the last words signed 'Ghost boy', with evidence from the pages.

ANALYSE

Explain how Rhodes uses methods — short sentences, inner thoughts, contrast, dialogue and the ghost motif — to show Jerome's fear and anger and the changing relationship between Jerome and Sarah.

EVALUATE

Judge whether Jerome's anger is justified and what his relationship with Sarah teaches us about empathy and allyship — and defend that judgement in WHAT/HOW/WHY paragraphs.

03

Key words

Learn these and use them in your writing — they are the words a strong response is built from.

prejudicejudging another person unfairly without knowing them
injusticewhen something is not fair
inequalitywhen a group does not have the same opportunities as others
stereotypea fixed belief about a whole group of people
discriminationtreated badly because of race, gender, religion or ability
racial profilingtreated unfairly or suspected just because of your race
empathythe ability to understand and share the feelings of another
allyshipacting on empathy: supporting others and speaking up
motifa repeated idea, image or symbol in a text
bearing witnesstelling the truth about injustice so it is remembered

How this unit is assessed

Assessment

End of unit assessment — week 7

One thematic question — What does the relationship between Jerome and Sarah teach us about empathy? — answered in 50 minutes, writing about the extract (pages 181–182, Jerome and Sarah talking about empathy) and about the novel as a whole. Six clear paragraphs with an introduction and a conclusion, each built WHAT/HOW/WHY, marked on the Emerging–Developing–Securing–Excelling ladder for AO1 (understanding themes and characters; insightful inference) and AO3 (using context). Lesson 15 is feedback and improvement (DIT) in purple pen.
