Non-fiction and Travel Writing

Six weeks of writing that is true: blogs, articles, speeches and, above all, travel writing about crossing a border into somewhere new. We learn the conventions of each form, analyse how travel writers use the senses and structure to take a reader with them, and write a full transactional piece of our own on the theme of journeys.

01

What you will learn

Three aims, climbing in difficulty. Every lesson in this unit works towards one of them.

UNDERSTAND

Name the main non-fiction forms — newspaper, blog, speech, article, travel writing — and the conventions each one uses.

ANALYSE

Explain how a travel writer's choices of sensory language, first-person voice and structure make a real place feel alive to a reader.

EVALUATE

Judge which choices work best for a given purpose and audience, and use them in a speech, article or travel blog of your own.

03

Key words

Learn these and use them in your writing — they are the words a strong response is built from.

non-fictionwriting about real people, places and events — not made up
conventiona feature readers expect from a form, like a headline in a news report
travel writingfirst-person non-fiction about a real journey to a real place
transactionalwriting that does a job in the real world: informs, persuades, argues
purposewhat a piece of writing is for — to inform, persuade, entertain, argue
audiencethe readers a writer has in mind, which shapes every choice
sensory languagewords that appeal to sight, sound, smell, taste and touch
first personwriting as 'I' and 'we' — the writer is inside the experience
retrievalfinding a fact that is stated in the text
inferenceworking out what the text suggests but does not say

05

How this unit is assessed

Assessment

Transactional writing — week 4

One full piece of transactional writing on the theme of journeys: a speech, an article or a travel blog about crossing a border into somewhere new. You will plan it in class with the purpose-and-audience grid and the moves we have practised, write it in silence, and get it back with two stars and a wish. Homework that week is to improve the draft.
