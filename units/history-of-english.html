The History of the English Language

Seven weeks that open Year 7 English with the big idea of Journeys: the journey of English itself, from the Celts and Romans to the Vikings, the Normans, the printing press and Shakespeare. Along the way we meet three famous British writers, learn what genre, form and a hero are, listen to the dialects that make each of us who we are, and build the words we will use for the rest of KS3.

01

What you will learn

Three aims, climbing in difficulty. Every lesson in this unit works towards one of them.

UNDERSTAND

Tell the story of how English grew, name the peoples and events that changed it, and explain what genre, form and hero mean.

ANALYSE

Work out where a word comes from and what its origin tells us; explain how a writer's time and place shape what they write.

EVALUATE

Judge, with evidence, which change did most to make English the language we speak — and defend that view in an analytical paragraph.

03

Key words

Learn these and use them in your writing — they are the words a strong response is built from.

etymologythe study of where a word comes from and how it has changed
Old Englishthe language of the Anglo-Saxons — the roots of our everyday words
Norsethe language of the Vikings; it gave us sky, egg, they and them
NormanFrench-speaking rulers who came in 1066 and gave us castle, beef, judge
borrowingtaking a word from another language and making it ours
genrea type of story or text, such as horror, romance or mystery
formthe shape a text takes — poem, play, novel, letter, article
contextthe time, place and life around a writer that shape what they write
dialectthe words and grammar people use in one place or group
accentthe way words sound when people from one place say them

How this unit is assessed

Assessment

Analytical paragraph — week 5

One analytical paragraph comparing two types of hero (epic, tragic or anti-hero), using a British literary example for each. You will plan it with the hero-types scaffold, write it in silence, and get it back with two stars and a wish before the dialects week. Week 1 also holds a short baseline assessment so we know where every reader starts.
