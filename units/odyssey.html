Unit 02 · HT2

The Odyssey

Six weeks with the oldest journey story in the West: a king leaves a war and spends ten years fighting monsters, gods and his own pride to get home. We read it as epic poetry and Greek myth, analyse how Odysseus is built as a hero, compare journeys across texts, and write a journey story of our own.

big
question

What makes a hero — and does a hero have to be good?

01

What you will learn

Three aims, climbing in difficulty. Every lesson in this unit works towards one of them.

UNDERSTAND

Retell the main episodes of Odysseus' journey and explain what an epic, a myth and an epic hero are.

ANALYSE

Explain how a writer's choices of language and structure make us admire, doubt or fear Odysseus.

EVALUATE

Judge, with evidence, whether Odysseus deserves to be called a hero — and defend that judgement in writing.

03

Key words

Learn these and use them in your writing — they are the words a strong response is built from.

epica long poem about a hero's great deeds and journeys
herothe main character of a story, admired for courage, skill or goodness — not always all three
hamartiathe flaw or mistake in a hero that leads to their downfall
hubrisdangerous pride — thinking you are greater than the gods or the rules
mythan ancient story about gods, heroes and monsters that explains how the world is
journeya long trip from one place to another; in stories, the hero is changed by it
narratorthe voice that tells the story
similea comparison using 'like' or 'as'
imagerywords that paint a picture for the senses
structurethe order a writer puts things in — and why

How this unit is assessed

Assessment

Extended writing — week 4

One side of comparative writing: how do two journey texts present their travellers, and which journey changes its hero more? You will plan it in class with the moves we have practised, write it in silence, and get it back with two stars and a wish before the narrative-writing week.
